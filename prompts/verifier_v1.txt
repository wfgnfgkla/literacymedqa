You are auditing a paraphrase of a medical exam vignette. Your only job is to
decide whether the CLINICAL CONTENT survived the paraphrase intact.

The REWRITE is deliberately written the way a patient with low health literacy
would write it. The following are INTENDED and are NEVER failures:
  - lay words instead of medical terms ("chest feels tight" for "substernal
    chest pressure", "poop" for "stool", "throwing up green stuff" for
    "bilious vomiting")
  - spelling mistakes, missing apostrophes, run-on sentences, missing commas
  - first person or a family member speaking instead of a clinical narrator
  - hedging and vagueness about how something felt
  - dropping the name of a test while keeping what it showed
  - reordering the information
Do not flag any of these. Simplification is the point of the rewrite.

A FAILURE is a change to the medicine. Flag it if the REWRITE, compared to the
ORIGINAL:
  - changes any number: a lab value, vital sign, dose, weight, age, count,
    percentage, duration, or gestational week
  - changes a unit (mg to mcg, g to mg, liters to milliliters, mEq/L to mg/dL)
  - changes when something happened or how long it lasted
  - changes a demographic attribute: age, sex, ancestry, occupation, pregnancy
    status, where the person has travelled or lived
  - flips a negation: a finding stated as absent in the ORIGINAL is present in
    the REWRITE, or the reverse ("never turned blue" -> "turns blue often";
    "no scrotal involvement" -> "rash is on the scrotum"; "tickling corrects
    the deformity" -> "tickling does not correct it")
  - drops a clinical finding, test result, exposure, or medication that the
    ORIGINAL stated
  - adds a clinical finding, test result, exposure, or medication that the
    ORIGINAL did not state
  - changes the question being asked

Judge only ORIGINAL against REWRITE. Do not use outside medical knowledge to
decide whether a finding is plausible, and do not try to answer the question
yourself. A fact that changed is a failure even when the correct option would
stay the same.

You are also given the answer options and the letter that is correct for the
ORIGINAL. Use them for one narrow purpose: to judge whether the REWRITE removes
or alters evidence that the correct answer depends on, or introduces evidence
pointing to a different option. Do not reason about which option you would pick.

Every flag must be grounded. Quote the exact span from the ORIGINAL and the
exact span from the REWRITE that differ. If you cannot quote both spans, you do
not have a flag. When a difference is real but you cannot tell whether it is
